About

Vertice helps companies buy and renew software at the best price, saving them millions. The platform enables users to track software and request new purchases or renewals. Since its creation, the system has been inflexible and not user-centric, which is where I came in. Designing the flow from first time onboarding to compliant approval processes.

Users

We have two main user groups in the overarching procurement process.

  • Vertice-supported negotiation → internal procurement manager and external client
  • Vertice as a platform → external procurement manager

Outcomes / Features

  • Onboarding
  • Global search across the platform, with intuitive navigation
  • Aligning the request page with our users’ goals and what they want to achieve
    • Then moving towards a flexible requisition model where the user starts with a business, category or employee need and follows a flexible route
  • Negotiation process with AI involvement
  • Developing a flexible approvals process

Problem

Our platform struggled to be user-centric and was too inflexible for many use cases. As a result, tracking in the platform was difficult. Users couldn’t do their job effectively, and the platform didn’t mirror the real-world flow, which made adoption a struggle.

Struggles

  • As we transformed our product, engineering created a brilliant system, but it came with challenges
  • Aligning the design with how engineering developed the system, and making it work within those constraints
  • At times the work spanned 80 engineers, with teams moving faster than design could cover, so part of the job was reworking AI-generated screens that had gone in ahead of a design pass

Research

  • Posthog, for user statistics, expanding on the search functionality to decrease time to value
  • User interviews with procurement managers inside and outside the company, to increase confidence in decision making and intuition for quick decisions
  • AI prototyping for quick feedback on features like the negotiation agent, helping validate the feeling of the platform

Learnings

  • Flexibility beats rigidity. It’s good for compliance and easy to draw up a rigid flow programmatically, but our users need forgiveness and flexibility. Building a flexible process improved the user experience 10-fold and made it much easier to track the entire process.
  • Learning the AI design process. With AI prototyping, it’s important that everything is clearly laid out. We need a clear vision of the user problem we’re trying to solve and what details we need to cover.
  • Making sure we cover each user use case and permissions is important in procurement, given data sensitivity.
  • Staying close to engineering is important, to make sure they have guidance when there isn’t enough design capacity to support them.
